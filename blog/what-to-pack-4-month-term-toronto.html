What to Pack for a 4-Month Term in Toronto: The Furnished-Room Packing List

A 4-month Toronto term is long enough to need real things, short enough that over-packing is a mistake. When your room is furnished, the list shrinks dramatically — but there are Toronto-specific items people always forget. Here's what to actually bring.

Why a Furnished Room Changes the List

If you've booked a furnished all-inclusive room — the sensible choice for a short term, as our 4-month budget guide explains — you can cross off almost everything heavy. No bed, mattress, desk, chair, lamps, kitchenware, or storage furniture; it's all there. That's the single biggest packing simplification, and it's why interns in furnished intern housing arrive with a suitcase, not a moving truck.

The list below assumes a furnished room with a shared, equipped kitchen (standard in co-living). If you're going unfurnished, your list triples — and so does your setup time and cost, which is the whole argument for furnished on a short stay.

Pack for your life, not your apartment. The apartment is handled.

The Essentials (Bring These)

Clothes for the season you'll span. A May-August term is shorts-and-light; a September-December term runs into real cold. Pack layers and don't under-estimate the shift. Personal electronics + chargers (Canada uses Type A/B plugs, 120V — bring an adapter if arriving from elsewhere). Documents: passport, study/work permit, offer letter, any ID — keep originals + digital copies. Toiletries + any prescription meds (bring enough; transferring prescriptions as a short-term newcomer is a hassle). A few personal/sentimental items to make the room yours — this matters more than people expect over four months alone in a new city.

That's genuinely most of it for a furnished room. Everything else you can buy in your first week once you see what you actually need.

The Toronto-Winter Items People Forget

If your term runs into October-March, Toronto gets genuinely cold (well below freezing, with wind and snow). Newcomers from warm climates consistently under-prepare and spend a miserable first cold week. Don't pack these from home if they're bulky — buy them here in your first week, but BUDGET for them: a proper winter coat (C$150-400), insulated waterproof boots (C$80-200), gloves, a warm hat, and a scarf. This is C$300-700 that surprises people; plan for it.

The flip side: don't haul heavy winter gear if your term is summer-only — you'll never use it and it eats your luggage. Match the gear to your actual term dates. Our relocation plan flags this as a week-one budget line for exactly this reason.

Rule of thumb: pack for the weather you'll arrive into, budget to buy for the weather you'll grow into.

What NOT to Pack

On a furnished short term, leave behind: bedding and towels (usually provided or cheaply bought here — check with your operator), kitchenware and small appliances (shared kitchen is equipped), furniture of any kind, bulk toiletries (buy here), and anything 'just in case' that you can acquire in a day in Toronto. Over-packing costs baggage fees and clutters a room you'll leave in four months.

The mental model: anything cheap and easy to buy in Toronto, and anything your furnished room already provides, stays home. Your luggage is for the irreplaceable (documents, meds, personal items) and the season-appropriate (clothes).

Confirm what your specific room includes before you finalize the list — a quick question to your operator ('are bedding and towels provided?') saves both over-packing and an uncomfortable first night. For what co-living rooms include, see our room types guide.

The 4-Month Packing Verdict

For a furnished Toronto term: pack clothes for your season(s), electronics + adapter, documents, meds, a few personal touches — and budget C$300-700 to buy winter gear here if your term runs cold. Leave the furniture, kitchenware, bedding, and bulk goods behind; your furnished room and a first-week shopping trip cover them.

This light-packing freedom is one of the quiet advantages of furnished all-inclusive housing for interns and co-op students — you arrive with a suitcase and are fully set up the same day, with none of the furniture-and-utilities scramble an unfurnished place demands.
