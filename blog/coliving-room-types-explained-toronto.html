Co-Living Room Types in Toronto Explained: Private, Deluxe & Master Suite

Co-living listings throw around 'private,' 'deluxe,' and 'master suite' as if everyone knows the difference. Here's a clear guide to what each Toronto co-living room type actually gives you, what it costs, and which one is worth it for your situation.

Why Room Type Matters More Than You Think

In co-living, the room type you choose shapes both your monthly cost and your daily experience more than the neighbourhood does. The spread between an entry private room and a master suite can be C$100+/week — real money over a year — and the difference in space, privacy, and amenities is significant. Choosing well means matching the type to how you actually live, not overpaying for a suite you don't need or cramming into a basic room you'll resent.

All Circle rooms share the fundamentals — furnished, all-inclusive (utilities and WiFi), a 4-month minimum stay, no credit check. The room type determines what's private to you versus shared. Here's how the tiers break down. For the full model, see our best coliving in Toronto guide.

Think of it as a privacy-and-space ladder: each step up trades more money for more of your own space.

Private / Standard Room: The Value Choice

The entry tier: your own private, furnished bedroom with shared kitchen, bathroom(s), and common areas. You get a locking private space to sleep, study, and retreat to, while sharing the practical rooms with housemates. This is co-living in its purest form — maximum community interaction, minimum cost.

It's the value choice, anchoring the lower end of the C$240-385/week range depending on neighbourhood (from C$240 at The Maddox). Best for: budget-conscious students, interns on short terms, and anyone who's out a lot and mainly needs a good private room to come home to. Browse private rooms in Toronto.

If you spend little time in your room and value the lowest cost plus the most community, the standard private room is the smart pick.

Deluxe Room: More Space, Same Sharing

A step up: a larger private room, often with better light, more storage, or a prime position in the building — but still sharing kitchen and bathroom with housemates. You're paying for a more comfortable private space while keeping the shared-amenity model.

Best for: people who spend more time in their room (remote workers, students who study at home, anyone who wants room for a proper desk and a bit of breathing space) and will pay a moderate premium for it. The extra space pays off if your room doubles as your workspace — an OCAD student storing materials, a remote-working young professional. See deluxe rooms in Toronto.

The deluxe tier is the sweet spot for anyone whose room is also where they work or study — the extra space earns its premium daily.

Master Suite: Private Bathroom & Maximum Privacy

The top tier: the largest rooms, typically with an ensuite/private bathroom and the most private layout in the building. You still get co-living's community and all-inclusive simplicity, but with a near-studio level of personal privacy — no bathroom sharing, the most space, the premium position.

Best for: young professionals who want privacy and comfort without the cost and setup of a solo apartment, anyone who values an ensuite highly, or people staying longer who want their space to feel fully their own. It anchors the upper end of the range (toward C$385/week at premium neighbourhoods). See master suite rooms in Toronto.

The master suite is co-living for people who'd otherwise rent a studio — they get privacy and an ensuite, but keep the flexibility, community, and all-inclusive pricing a solo lease can't match. Our co-living vs apartment comparison runs that trade-off.

Which Room Type Is Worth It For You

The rule: match the type to how much time you spend in your room and how much you value privacy. Private/standard — out a lot, budget-first, want maximum community: the value pick. Deluxe — your room is also your workspace, you want space and light, moderate premium: the sweet spot. Master suite — you want an ensuite and maximum privacy, you'd otherwise rent a studio, longer stay: the premium that beats a solo apartment.

Don't overpay for privacy you won't use, and don't cram if your room is your daily workspace. All three keep co-living's core benefits — furnished, all-inclusive, flexible, community.
