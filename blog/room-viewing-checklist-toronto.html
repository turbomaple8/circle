The Toronto Room Viewing Checklist: 15 Things to Check Before You Sign

Photos lie, listings exaggerate, and the friendly person showing you a room has every incentive to rush you. A proper viewing — in person or on a thorough video call — is your one chance to catch the problems that make month two miserable. Here's the 15-point checklist.

Before the Viewing: Three Things to Confirm

1. Verify who you're meeting. Match the name to ID and, ideally, to the property. The most expensive Toronto room mistakes aren't bad rooms — they're rooms rented by someone who doesn't own them. Our scam prevention guide documents the fake-leaseholder pattern.

2. Confirm it's the actual room. 'We'll show you an identical unit upstairs' is the oldest bait-and-switch there is. Insist on seeing the exact room you'd rent.

3. Go at the right time. View in daylight to judge natural light, but if you work nights or study late, also ask about evening noise. A room that's peaceful at 2 p.m. can sit above a bar that opens at 9.

At the Viewing: The Room Itself (Points 4-8)

4. Water pressure and hot water. Run the shower. Then run a tap elsewhere and check the shower again — weak recovery means cold morning showers when housemates are up. 5. Window orientation. North-facing stays dark and cool; west-facing bakes in summer afternoons; a north light through a Toronto winter is genuinely depressing for some people. 6. Phone signal in the actual bedroom. Basement rooms especially can be dead zones. 7. Lock on the bedroom door. Non-negotiable in a shared house. 8. Heating and cooling. Ask specifically: is there AC for July, and who controls the thermostat in January? The thermostat war is real.

These physical checks are exactly what an all-inclusive, professionally furnished room removes from your worry list — the room is set up to a standard rather than to whatever a private landlord bothered with. Compare the models in our furnished vs unfurnished maths.

The Shared Spaces (Points 9-12)

9. Kitchen capacity per person. How many people share this fridge and stove? Four is workable; eight is a daily conflict. 10. Bathroom ratio. One bathroom for five people means a morning queue — ask the real number, not the optimistic one. 11. Cleanliness of common areas right now. What you see on the viewing is the best it will ever look. If it's rough today, it's rough always. 12. Laundry. In-unit, in-building, or 'a laundromat nearby'? The last one costs you an afternoon a week.

In a managed co-living home, common areas are professionally cleaned on a schedule and capacity is designed rather than accidental — the structural reason shared-house friction is lower. See how it works on our best coliving in Toronto guide.

The Housemates (Point 13 — The One People Skip)

13. Meet at least one current housemate. Not the person collecting rent — an actual resident. Ask them one question: 'What's the most annoying thing about living here?' Their answer, and how freely they give it, tells you more than the entire rest of the viewing. A house where you're not allowed to talk to residents is hiding something.

If you're assembling your own roommate group rather than joining an existing house, the compatibility work moves earlier — our roommates in Toronto guide and the 12 compatibility questions cover exactly what to screen for.

The Paperwork (Points 14-15)

14. Get it in writing. A written agreement naming the exact room, the rate, what's included, the deposit terms, and the notice period. 'We'll sort the details later' means the details will be sorted against you. 15. Pay traceably. E-transfer with a description line, never cash, never wire, never gift cards. In Ontario your protections depend heavily on your documented status — a paper trail is the difference between recourse and a lost deposit.

Run all fifteen and you'll catch the problems that turn a promising room into a miserable year. Or skip the audition entirely: a furnished, all-inclusive room booked on a live video viewing with a written agreement and no credit check removes most of this list by design. Doing It Remotely: The Video Viewing Checklist

If you're booking from another city or country — common for international students and interns — you can't attend in person, but a thorough video viewing covers most of the same ground. Insist on a live video call, not pre-recorded clips (which can show a different unit). Ask the person to walk the exact room slowly: pan the window and tell you the direction it faces, run the shower on camera, show the kitchen and count the housemates' shelves in the fridge, walk to the nearest transit stop timing it live.

Red flags on a remote viewing are the same but sharper: refusal to do live video, 'the tenant is sleeping so I can only show you photos,' or pressure to send a deposit before the call. A legitimate operator does live video without hesitation and accepts e-transfer with a written agreement first. Anything else, walk away — the remote-booking scam patterns are in our scam prevention guide.

The alternative that removes the remote-viewing risk entirely: book a furnished, all-inclusive room from an operator with a verifiable address, real reviews, and a standard application — the whole point of a managed co-living home is that you're not gambling on one stranger's honesty over a video call.
