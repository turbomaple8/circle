Intern Commute Maths in Toronto: TTC vs Walking to Work

On a 4-month internship, your commute runs 80+ times — and the wrong choice quietly taxes your time, money, and energy every single day. Here's the honest maths on TTC versus walking to your Toronto placement, and why interns who can walk usually should.

Why Commute Maths Matters More for Interns

A permanent employee amortises a bad commute over years and adapts. An intern has 16 weeks — no time to adapt, every inefficiency felt immediately, and a placement intense enough that reclaimed time and energy matter more than usual. The commute decision is really a housing decision, made before you arrive, that you live with for the whole term.

Two options dominate for a downtown Toronto placement: ride the TTC from a cheaper, farther room, or pay a bit more to live walkable to the office. The maths below shows when each wins — but the short version is that walkability's advantages compound over a short, demanding term.

Our internship housing guide covers the full picture; this zooms into the commute line specifically.

The TTC Option: Real Cost and Time

The TTC monthly pass is C$128 (C$122.45 at the post-secondary rate if you're a co-op student) — roughly C$500 over a 16-week term. That's the visible cost. The hidden costs are time and reliability: a typical 20-30 minute each-way subway-plus-walk commute is 40-60 minutes a day, ~6-7 hours a week, 100+ hours over the term.

Reliability is the other tax. Line delays, shuttle buses, winter platform waits, and rush-hour crowding all land on exactly the days you can least afford them — the early pre-round start, the late finish after a deadline. A TTC commute is fine; it's just rarely free in the ways that matter to a stretched intern.

TTC makes sense when your placement is genuinely far from affordable housing, or when your room is dramatically cheaper than any walkable option — the farther-and-cheaper trade can net out positive. Run it against your actual numbers.

The Walking Option: What It Actually Buys

A walkable room — within ~15 minutes of your placement — costs more in rent but zeroes the transit line and returns the commute hours. No C$500 pass, no delays, no winter platform, and 100+ hours of your term back. For a finance intern on Bay Street (our Bay Street guide) or a tech intern in King West (tech guide), walkable housing often costs only C$60-90/week more than a farther room — and that premium is frequently cost-neutral once you subtract the pass and value the time.

Walking also adds the things that don't show in a spreadsheet: daily exercise, zero commute stress, the ability to go home for a real lunch, and being minutes away when a late night runs long. On a demanding term, those compound into meaningfully better weeks.

Circle's downtown residences are chosen for exactly this — The York two minutes from Union, The Yonge central to everything. See intern housing in Toronto.

The Crossover: When TTC Wins

Walking isn't always the answer. TTC wins when: your placement is far from any affordable walkable room (some suburban or east-end sites), your walkable options cost C$150+/week more than a transit-accessible room (premium neighbourhood tax), your schedule is regular 9-5 (less exposed to delay-sensitive early/late trips), or you actively want to live in a different neighbourhood than you work and don't mind the ride.

In these cases, the farther-cheaper-plus-pass math can beat paying the walkable premium. The honest rule: compute the all-in weekly difference (walkable rent premium vs. farther rent + C$30/week pass), then weigh the ~6-7 hours/week of reclaimed time at whatever your time is worth. If the premium is small and the time saved is real, walk. If the premium is steep, ride.

The subway premium guide runs the parallel math for subway proximity specifically.

The Intern Commute Verdict

For most downtown Toronto internships, walkable housing wins: the rent premium is usually modest, the pass cost disappears, and the 100+ reclaimed hours plus the energy and reliability gains compound over a short, intense term. Pay the walkable premium when it's small; ride the TTC when the premium is steep or your placement is genuinely far.

Either way, decide by mapping your real daily trip to your actual office address — not by a vague sense of 'near downtown.'
