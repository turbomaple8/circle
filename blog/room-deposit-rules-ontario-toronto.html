Room Deposit Rules in Ontario: What a Landlord Can (and Can't) Legally Ask For

"Two months' deposit, cash, before you see the room" — if a Toronto room landlord says this, they're breaking Ontario law. Most room-renters don't know the deposit rules, so they overpay or get scammed. Here's exactly what a landlord can and can't legally ask for.

Why Deposit Rules Are Worth Knowing

Ontario law is fairly clear and fairly protective on deposits — but informal room arrangements routinely ignore it, sometimes out of ignorance and sometimes to test whether you know your rights. Knowing the rules protects your money directly: it tells you which asks are legitimate, which to push back on, and which are outright red flags for a scam.

This builds on our Ontario renter rights primer and the payment-safety mechanics in our e-transfer guide. Note one caveat up front: if you share a kitchen/bathroom with the OWNER, you may fall in the RTA-exempt grey zone where some rules don't apply (our owner-vs-tenant guide) — know your category.

This is a plain-language explainer, not legal advice. For a dispute, consult a tenant legal clinic or the LTB.

What a Landlord CAN Legally Ask For

For most covered residential tenancies in Ontario, a landlord can require: Last month's rent deposit — a rent deposit (not a damage deposit) applied to your final month, capped at one month's rent (or one week's for weekly tenancies). It must be used for the last month and the landlord owes you interest on it annually. A key/fob deposit — refundable, and limited to the actual replacement cost of the keys (not an arbitrary 'security' sum).

That's essentially the complete legal list for upfront deposits. A landlord asking for first + last is normal and legal. A landlord asking for a reasonable key deposit is legal. Anything beyond that enters forbidden territory.

When you pay a legal deposit, pay by e-transfer with a clear memo ('Last month's rent deposit — Room X, [address]') and keep the record — your paper trail if a dispute arises.

What a Landlord CANNOT Legally Ask For

For most covered tenancies, these are NOT permitted: A separate 'damage deposit' or 'security deposit' beyond last month's rent — Ontario does not allow it for most residential tenancies, full stop. More than one month's rent as deposit — 'two or three months upfront' exceeds the legal cap. A non-refundable 'cleaning' or 'admin' fee dressed up as a deposit. Pet deposits — not permitted in Ontario.

A landlord who demands a large cash 'security deposit,' multiple months upfront, or a non-refundable fee is either uninformed about Ontario law or testing you — and if it's combined with cash-only, no-viewing, or no-written-agreement, it's a scam signature (see our red flags catalogue).

Push back: 'Ontario only permits last month's rent as a deposit — I can pay first and last by e-transfer with an agreement.' A legitimate landlord accepts this; a scammer evaporates.

The International-Student Pressure Point

International students are the most-targeted group for illegal deposit demands, precisely because they're new, don't know Ontario rules, and face the credit-check wall that makes 'just pay 6 months upfront and skip the credit check' sound like a solution. It isn't — large prepayment to a private individual you've never met, sight-unseen, from abroad, is exactly the scam scenario, and it's also not legally required.

The legitimate newcomer path doesn't demand illegal deposits: operators built for international students require no credit check and no guarantor, charge only the legal deposit, and accept e-transfer with a written agreement. Our no-credit-check housing guide covers this.

If any landlord frames a large upfront prepayment as 'how it works for international students,' treat it as a red flag, not a norm.

The Deposit Rules Verdict

Ontario lets a landlord ask for last month's rent (capped at one month) plus a refundable key deposit at cost — and essentially nothing else upfront for most tenancies. Separate damage/security deposits, multiple months upfront, non-refundable fees, and pet deposits are NOT permitted. Know this, and you'll recognize both the honest ask and the illegal one.

Always pay deposits by traceable e-transfer with a memo, get a written agreement naming the deposit terms, and never pay before seeing the exact room live. For the firmest footing, managed co-living and per-room rentals charge only legal deposits with standard agreements. Pair this with the renter rights primer and e-transfer safety guide.
