The 12 Roommate Compatibility Questions, Explained: What Each Answer Really Tells You

Our roommates guide lists twelve questions to ask before signing with a stranger. This deep-dive explains what each answer actually reveals — and the specific phrasings that should end the conversation.

Why Structured Questions Beat Vibes

Everyone believes they can read people. Toronto's roommate horror stories are written by people who believed that, met someone charming for forty minutes, and signed. Structured questions work because they force specifics — and specifics are where incompatibility hides. A person can perform 'chill' for a viewing; they cannot improvise a detailed, consistent answer about how they split a hydro bill dispute in their last house.

The twelve questions from our roommates in Toronto pillar cluster into four groups: daily rhythm (sleep, work, guests), money mechanics, conflict style, and exit terms. Here's how to score the answers.

Daily Rhythm: Listen for Specifics, Not Adjectives

When you ask about cleaning, 'I'm pretty clean' is a non-answer — everyone self-rates clean. A real answer sounds like: 'I do my dishes same-day, vacuum my room weekly, and I'd want a shared-area rota.' Specificity signals someone who has actually lived with people and thought about the machinery.

Guest policy answers reveal the most. Red-flag phrasing: 'my partner stays over sometimes' — press for the number, because 'sometimes' has meant five nights a week in a thousand broken roommate arrangements. A fair answer names a frequency and offers reciprocity.

Schedule questions are about collision, not judgement: a night-shift nurse and a 6 a.m. gym person can be perfect roommates in a well-insulated house and terrible ones in a house with creaky floors. Ask about the house, not just the human.

Money Mechanics: The Apps Test

Ask how they split costs and listen for a system: Splitwise, a shared spreadsheet, e-transfer on the first. Any system beats 'we just figure it out' — 'figuring it out' is where resentment compounds at 19% APR.

The deposit question is a scam filter as much as a compatibility one. If you're joining someone else's lease, verify they're actually on it before money moves — our scam prevention guide documents the fake-leaseholder pattern that peaks every September. Legitimate people show paperwork without flinching; that's the whole test.

And the Ontario reality check from our pillar guide applies: your legal standing depends on your documented status, so 'what's my exact arrangement here' is not a rude question — it's the question.

Conflict Style: The Only Question That Predicts Year Two

'How do you handle disagreements?' produces rehearsed answers, so ask the behavioural version: 'Tell me about a conflict in your last shared house and how it ended.' People narrate their actual pattern — listen for whether their story contains any self-awareness or whether every previous roommate was somehow the villain.

The universal-villain narrative is the single strongest predictor of your future: you will be the next villain. Conversely, someone who says 'we had a dishes standoff, it got passive-aggressive, eventually we made a rota and it was fine' has demonstrated repair — the only skill that matters at month eight.

This is also the honest pitch for professionally matched housing: at Circle, compatibility screening plus a management layer for disputes means the month-eight conversation happens with a neutral third party instead of on a whiteboard of grievances. See how matching works on our best coliving page — or if you're building your own house, at minimum run the behavioural question on every candidate.

For the room itself — pricing, agreements, what to verify before paying — pair this guide with rent a room in Toronto and the furnished maths deep-dive.

Exit Terms: The Question Pair That Protects You Both

Questions eleven and twelve — length of stay and early-exit handling — are the ones people skip because they feel pessimistic at a first meeting. Ask them anyway. Misaligned horizons are a slow-motion conflict: the person planning eight months and the person planning three years will make different decisions about furniture, about effort, about resolving the small stuff, from week one.

A good exit answer includes mechanics: notice period, who finds the replacement, what happens to the deposit, whether the departing person's name comes off utilities. 'We'd figure it out' means the person leaving first wins and the person staying inherits the mess. In Ontario, this is doubly important because informal roommates often lack tenancy protections — the written-agreement rule from our pillar guide is what turns a handshake into a plan.

Score all twelve answers and a pattern emerges within twenty minutes — specific, systemised, self-aware people cluster their answers; improvisers improvise everywhere. Match with the first kind, or skip the audition entirely and let a matching process do it: that trade-off, honestly weighed, is the entire co-living value proposition in one sentence.
