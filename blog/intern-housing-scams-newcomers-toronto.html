Intern Housing Scams in Toronto: How Newcomers Get Targeted (and How to Avoid It)

Incoming interns are among the most-targeted victims of Toronto rental scams — you're booking remotely, on a deadline, often from another country, with no way to walk over and check a place in person. Scammers know this. Here are the specific scams aimed at newcomer interns and exactly how to avoid them.

Why Interns Are Prime Targets

Scammers target the conditions that make you vulnerable, and an incoming intern checks every box: you're booking from abroad or another city so you can't view in person, you're on a tight timeline tied to a placement start date, you lack local knowledge of fair prices and neighbourhoods, and you're often eager and trusting because you're excited about the opportunity. That combination is exactly what a rental scammer looks for.

The pressure of a fixed internship start date is the key lever. A scammer's whole game is urgency — 'this room will be gone, send the deposit now' — and an intern with a hard start date feels that urgency for real. It makes you more likely to skip the checks that would protect you.

Understanding that you're a target isn't paranoia — it's the first defence. Once you know scammers are specifically hunting people in your exact situation, the warning signs become obvious.

The Scams That Target Interns Specifically

The remote-only listing: a great room at a good price, but the 'landlord' is conveniently abroad and can only mail you the keys after you wire a deposit. The room either doesn't exist or isn't theirs to rent. This is the number-one intern scam.

The fake-urgency deposit: 'Several other interns are interested, secure it today with an e-transfer.' Real urgency exists in peak season, but a legitimate landlord still lets you view before paying. Manufactured urgency is a scam tool.

The hijacked listing: scammers copy a real listing's photos and details, undercut the price, and collect deposits from multiple victims for a room they don't control. If a price seems well below the market our price decoder describes, be suspicious.

The overseas-agent scam: a fake 'housing agent' who'll 'handle everything' for a fee, targeting international interns unfamiliar with how Toronto renting works. Legitimate operators don't need an intermediary charging you upfront.

The Defences That Stop All of Them

Every intern scam depends on you skipping one or more of these checks. Do them and you're safe: Never send money before a live video viewing of the exact room, with the person on camera walking the space. Verify the person and property — name matching ID and, where possible, a verifiable connection to the address. Always get a written agreement naming the room, rate and terms before paying. Pay only by traceable e-transfer — never wire, gift cards, or crypto. Refuse to be rushed — a real room survives you taking a day.

The single most powerful rule: no money before a live viewing of the exact room. A scammer physically cannot satisfy this — they don't have the room to show you live. Insisting on it defeats nearly every intern scam at once. The full anti-scam sequence is in our e-transfer safety guide and the complete scam prevention guide.

The Safe Path for Remote-Booking Interns

Because interns often genuinely need to book remotely, the safest approach isn't 'view everything in person' — it's to book from a verifiable operator where the anti-scam checks are structurally satisfied. A legitimate co-living operator has a real, verifiable business address, real reviews from real residents, a standard written agreement, and does live video viewings without hesitation. You're dealing with a known entity, not a stranger's texts.

That's the structural reason so many international interns choose managed intern housing: it removes the exact conditions scams exploit. No credit check, no guarantor, live video viewing, e-transfer with a proper agreement, 48-hour approval — the safe checklist is just how it works, not something you have to fight for. See the model on our best coliving page.

Whether you go that route or find a room independently, apply the same standard: verifiable, viewable, documented, traceable, unrushed.

The Bottom Line for Newcomer Interns

You're a target — accept that, and it becomes a strength, because you'll see the scams coming. Every intern rental scam relies on you being remote, rushed, and trusting enough to skip verification. Refuse to skip it: no money before a live viewing of the exact room, verify the person and property, get it in writing, pay traceably, and never let a fake deadline rush you.

A lost deposit to a scammer is a brutal way to start an internship in a new country. It's also entirely avoidable with a few non-negotiable rules. Book from a verifiable operator or hold any independent landlord to the same standard, and you'll arrive to a real room and a great start.
