Tech Intern Housing in King West: Where to Actually Live for a Four-Month Placement

You got the offer, the office is on King West, and every instinct says live on King West. That instinct will cost you roughly a month of your internship income and probably fail anyway. Here is what a four-month tech placement actually needs from a Toronto neighbourhood.

Why King West Became the Default Answer

King West earned its reputation honestly. The stretch running west from the Financial District through the Entertainment District and toward Liberty Village holds a genuine density of software companies, scale-ups and the satellite offices of firms headquartered elsewhere. If your internship offer names an address between Bathurst and University, you are in that corridor, and your first search will be for a King West apartment.

The problem is that the corridor is roughly two kilometres long and almost entirely served by one streetcar line and a fifteen-minute walk to two subway stations. The postcode is not what gets you to a 9:30 standup. Proximity does — and proximity in downtown Toronto is measured in minutes and transfers, not in whether your lease says King Street West on it.

Our intern housing in Toronto guide makes the broader case; this piece is specifically about the King West corridor and what a sixteen-week term can realistically buy near it.

What a King West Lease Actually Demands

Walk into a standard condo rental in the corridor and the requirements are consistent: a twelve-month term, a credit check, first and last month's rent up front, proof of employment income, and an empty unit. Every one of those is a problem for an intern.

The twelve-month term is the headline issue. You need sixteen weeks. Landlords are not being difficult — they are optimising for a full year, and a four-month tenant is a worse deal at any price. The credit check is the second wall: if you have been in Canada for eight months, or you are a student with no Canadian credit file, there is nothing to check. Some landlords will accept a guarantor, which means asking a parent in another country to underwrite a lease they cannot read.

Then the unit is empty. Not lightly furnished — empty. Bed, desk, chair, lamp, kitchenware, shower curtain, wifi installation with a thirty-day activation window. For a sixteen-week stay you are buying furniture you will resell at a loss in week fifteen, on a Saturday, to strangers from a marketplace app.

The Sixteen-Week Problem Nobody Prices

Interns budget rent. They rarely budget the cost of the term mismatch, which is where the money actually goes. If a twelve-month lease is the only option and you need four months, one of three things happens: you pay for months you do not use, you sublet and absorb the risk of the sublet failing, or you take an informal arrangement with no paperwork and no protection.

The third option is the one most interns choose, and it is where Toronto's internship season produces its casualties every May and September. An arrangement with no lease means no legal standing, no documented deposit, and no recourse when the person who took your e-transfer stops answering. Our scam prevention guide documents the specific patterns that spike during intake season, and the fake-sublet is the one built for exactly your situation.

The honest reframe: your constraint is not budget. It is term length. Solve the term and the rest of the problem gets much smaller.

The Walkable Alternative: Queen West and the Waterfront

Circle does not have a house on King West, and pretending otherwise would be useless to you. What we have is two locations that sit on either side of the corridor and put you inside it on foot or on a single streetcar.

The Queen sits in Queen West, one block north of the King corridor's western half. If your office is between Bathurst and Spadina, this is a walk, not a commute. The neighbourhood itself — covered in our co-living in Queen West page — is the part of downtown that stays interesting after the offices empty, which matters more than interns expect when they arrive knowing nobody.

The York sits in the Waterfront and Financial District, which puts you at the corridor's eastern end and next to Union. If your office is closer to University or your team is in one of the bank towers, the Waterfront and Financial District is the better anchor. Both run from C$240 to C$385 a week, furnished, with a one-month minimum term and no credit check — which is the same sentence as "the term problem is solved."

Commute Maths: Minutes, Not Kilometres

The 504 King streetcar runs the length of the corridor and is, on a normal weekday morning, the fastest way across it. From Queen West you are one short walk north-to-south from any King West office between Bathurst and Spadina. From the Waterfront you are walking or riding a handful of stops.

Compare that against the alternative interns default into: a cheaper room in the suburbs, forty-five to sixty minutes each way, two transfers, in February. That is ten hours a week. Over sixteen weeks it is roughly one hundred and sixty hours — four full working weeks of your internship spent standing on a platform. Interns take that trade because the weekly number looks smaller, and then spend the term too tired to do the networking that was the actual point of coming.

If you are weighing the wider downtown options rather than the corridor specifically, co-living in the Downtown Core covers the middle ground between the two.

What to Verify Before You Send a Deposit

Whatever you choose, run the same checks. Confirm the person taking your money has the legal right to rent the space — ask directly, and watch the reaction rather than the answer. Get the total move-in cost itemised in writing before anything moves, including any deposit, and understand that in Ontario a last month's rent deposit is permitted while a separate damage deposit is not.

Ask what the exit looks like. A four-month placement has a hard end date; the arrangement should have a matching one, written down. If the answer is a shrug, you have found the problem early, which is the cheapest time to find it.

See the room, or see a live video of the specific room — not the building, not a render, not a photo from a listing that appears on three sites at three prices. Our complete intern housing guide has the longer checklist; these three are the ones that catch most of it.

The Honest Trade-off

Living on King West itself, in your own unit, with a twelve-month lease and furniture you chose, is a real thing you can want. It is also a poor fit for sixteen weeks, and the version of it available to an intern is usually a sublet from a stranger with no paperwork.

The trade you are actually offered is this: a private, furnished room in a managed house one neighbourhood over, on a term that matches your placement, with people who arrived the same month you did — or a solo unit at the exact address, at a cost and risk profile that was designed for someone with a permanent job. Interns who pick the first one spend their term in the corridor. Interns who hold out for the second one often spend it commuting from Scarborough.
