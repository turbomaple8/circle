Roommates in Toronto: How to Find Good Ones — Or Skip the Search Entirely

The honest guide to finding compatible roommates in Toronto, and what to do when you'd rather not gamble.

Every September and January, thousands of people search for roommates in Toronto — scrolling Facebook groups, Kijiji threads, and roommate-finder apps, trying to guess from three photos and a paragraph whether a stranger will respect quiet hours and do their dishes. Sometimes it works out. Often it doesn't. This guide covers both paths: how to vet potential roommates properly if you go the traditional route, and how coliving quietly solved the roommate problem for people who'd rather not leave it to chance.

Where People Actually Find Roommates in Toronto

Facebook groups. The biggest volume — groups like "Toronto Roommates & Rooms for Rent" have tens of thousands of members. The upside is choice; the downside is zero vetting. Scam listings, ghost profiles, and bait-and-switch rooms are common enough that our housing scam prevention guide exists largely because of them.

Roommate-finder apps. Apps add profile filters and some identity friction, which helps. But matching on "likes hiking, age 22-28" tells you nothing about the things that actually break houses: cleaning standards, guest policies, noise tolerance, and how someone behaves during a disagreement.

University housing boards. If you're a student, your school's off-campus housing board is safer than open marketplaces — listings are at least tied to a student account. Availability is thin outside the pre-September rush, though, and quality still varies widely. For a broader look at the student options, see our student housing in Toronto guide.

Word of mouth. Statistically the best outcomes — a friend-of-a-friend has reputational skin in the game. But you can't schedule serendipity, and most newcomers to Toronto don't have the network yet.

12 Questions to Ask a Potential Roommate Before You Sign Anything

Compatibility isn't about becoming best friends — it's about aligned expectations. These twelve questions surface the friction points before they become 2 a.m. arguments:

  1. What does "clean" mean to you — and how often do you actually clean shared spaces?
  2. What are your work/study hours? Early riser or night owl?
  3. How do you feel about overnight guests, and how often?
  4. How do you handle disagreements — talk it out same-day, or need space first?
  5. What's your policy on borrowing food, dishes, or anything else?
  6. How do you split shared costs — apps, spreadsheets, or "roughly even"?
  7. Do you smoke, vape, or use cannabis at home?
  8. Pets: have any, want any, allergic to any?
  9. How long do you plan to stay? What happens if one of us leaves early?
  10. Whose name goes on the lease and utilities — and what does that mean legally for the other person?
  11. What temperature do you keep the thermostat at? (Seriously — this ends friendships.)
  12. Can we do a video call or meet twice before deciding?

If a potential roommate dodges half of these or pressures you to decide same-day, that's your answer.

The Legal Side Most Roommates Skip

In Ontario, roommates who aren't on the lease often have far fewer protections than they assume. If you're subletting from a leaseholder, your housing security depends on their relationship with the landlord. If you're paying cash to someone whose name you've never verified, you may have no paper trail at all when something goes wrong.

Minimum paper trail for any roommate arrangement: a written agreement naming the room, the rate, what's included, and the notice period — plus payment by e-transfer (never cash) so there's a record. It feels awkward to ask a friendly stranger for paperwork. It feels worse to lose a month's rent with no recourse.

The roommate hunt is just the risky, unpaid work of trying to manufacture that outcome from strangers on the internet.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I find roommates in Toronto safely?+

Verify identity before viewing, meet in person or on video at least twice, never pay cash or send deposits before seeing the room, insist on a written agreement, and pay by e-transfer for a paper trail. University housing boards and word of mouth are safer than open marketplaces.

How much does a room with roommates cost in Toronto?+

A room in a shared downtown apartment typically runs C$900–1,400/month plus your share of utilities and any furniture costs. Coliving at Circle starts at C$240/week (about C$960/month) with utilities, WiFi and furniture included.

What's the difference between finding roommates and coliving?+

Finding roommates means you do the vetting, matching, bill-splitting and conflict resolution yourself. Coliving means a professional operator does the matching, bundles the bills, maintains the shared spaces and mediates conflicts. Same social outcome, different risk profile.

Am I protected legally if I'm not on the lease in Ontario?+

Often less than you'd think. Occupants who aren't on the lease may not have full tenant protections, and cash arrangements leave no paper trail. Always get a written agreement naming your room, rate and notice period, and pay by e-transfer.

Can I choose who I live with at Circle?+

Residents are matched by compatibility — schedule, life stage, and expectations around noise, guests and cleanliness. You can also apply together with a friend and request the same home, subject to availability across our four Toronto locations.
